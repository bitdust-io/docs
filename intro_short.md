BitDust is a distributed online storage network, in which only the data owner can read the data, and decides in advance which data can be shared with trusted contacts.

Imagine a situation when two friends and you agree to help each other store data. On your computer you store the data that both of your friends uploaded to you via the Internet, and you in turn can use the free space on their machines to save your files. This creates redundancy, but allows important data to be stored more safely. It is also a fair deal, because all three are interested in the maximum safety and availability of the stored information. BitDust works in a similar way, but on the scale of the whole network: your data is stored on randomly selected nodes, not necessarily on those who store their data on your computer - see [Sharing resources](storage.md#sharing-resources).

The BitDust network is a voluntary association of people sharing resources of their personal computers. Their machines run compatible copies of the BitDust program, which follow the same BitDust protocol and enable all device communications. Data is transmitted from one user to another and stored on the users' hard disk drives.

BitDust also protects your privacy - the software does not collect any personal data, you are known in the network only by a nickname you choose, and all your data and messages are encrypted.

BitDust is an open-source project of a worldwide distributed peer-to-peer network that aims to provide online storage, safe personal communications and full control of your own private data.

By design, the BitDust network strives to avoid any centralization - your device communicates with other devices in the network peer-to-peer via digitally signed packets, in which all your private data is encrypted (see [Data Security](security.md#data-transfer)). The BitDust maintainers do not govern, account or monitor the users - there is no central server through which this could be done. The only checks in the network are made by the nodes themselves: your software monitors the availability of your own suppliers (see [Automatic Data Rebuilding](rebuilding.md)). The built-in public blockchain records storage contracts and payments between nodes, as well as identity registrations (nickname and public key) - it does not contain your personal data; at the moment this does not affect the operation of the network in any way, and the function can be disabled in the program settings (see [Blockchain and storage contracts](storage.md#blockchain-and-storage-contracts)).

This story is about respecting our digital freedoms and making people as independent as possible. It may sound far too optimistic, but that is my lifestyle: all or nothing! You can read more about the project at [www.bitdust.io](https://bitdust.io).
